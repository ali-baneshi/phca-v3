# Glossary — Controlled Vocabulary

**File:** glossary.md
**Status:** COMPLETE

---

## A

**Active Inference**
The process by which an agent acts to fulfill its predictions — changing the environment to match its internal model. From the Free Energy Principle (Friston).

**ACT-R** (Adaptive Control of Thought — Rational)
A hybrid symbolic-subsymbolic cognitive architecture developed by John R. Anderson. Models cognition as a production system modulated by subsymbolic equations.

**Agent**
An autonomous entity that perceives its environment, processes information, and acts to achieve goals.

**Attention**
The selective processing of information due to limited processing capacity. Neuroscience distinguishes selective, divided, sustained, and executive attention.

**Autopoiesis**
Self-production — the defining property of living systems (Maturana & Varela). The claim that autopoietic systems are inherently cognitive.

**Axiom**
A universal principle that appears to hold for all intelligent systems, regardless of substrate.

## B

**Bayesian Brain**
The hypothesis that the brain represents information probabilistically and updates beliefs via Bayesian inference.

**BDI** (Belief-Desire-Intention)
An agent architecture that separates reasoning into beliefs (world knowledge), desires (goals), and intentions (committed plans).

**Biased Competition Theory**
The dominant neurobiological framework for attention: multiple stimuli compete for neural representation, and competition is resolved by top-down (goal-driven) and bottom-up (salience-driven) biasing signals.

**Bounded Rationality**
The principle that rational decision-making is limited by finite time, computation, and information (Simon).

## C

**CAP Theorem**
A distributed systems theorem stating that a system cannot simultaneously guarantee Consistency, Availability, and Partition Tolerance.

**Catastrophic Forgetting**
A failure mode where learning new information destroys previously learned knowledge.

**Chunking** (Soar)
A learning mechanism that automatically creates new productions by summarizing problem-solving episodes.

**CoALA** (Cognitive Architectures for Language Agents)
A conceptual framework for building LLM-based agents using classical cognitive architecture principles (Sumers et al., 2023).

**Cognitive Architecture**
A fixed structure for intelligent agents that specifies memory systems, processing mechanisms, and interaction patterns.

**Cognitive Cycle** (LIDA)
The fundamental unit of cognition in LIDA: perception → conscious broadcast → action selection, occurring at ~10 Hz.

**Consciousness (Functional)**
In LIDA, the computational process by which information wins competition for global broadcast. Does not address subjective experience (qualia).

**Consensus**
A distributed systems mechanism for multiple nodes to agree on a single value despite failures.

**Control Theory**
The study of feedback systems that maintain desired states despite disturbances.

**Criticality**
The regime between order and chaos where information processing is hypothesized to be optimal.

## D

**Data Processing Inequality**
An information theory theorem stating that processing cannot increase information content.

**Declarative Memory**
Memory for facts and events that can be consciously recalled (explicit memory). Divided into episodic (events) and semantic (facts).

**Distributed Cognition**
The theory that cognitive processes extend beyond individual minds to include tools, artifacts, and other agents.

**Distributional Shift**
A failure mode where the system encounters data outside its training distribution.

## E

**Edge of Chaos**
The phase transition between order and chaos where complex systems exhibit maximal information processing capacity.

**Embodied Cognition**
The theory that cognitive processes are shaped by the body's physical characteristics and interactions with the environment.

**Emergence**
The appearance of properties at a system level that are not present in the system's individual components.

**Episodic Memory**
Memory for specific events and experiences in temporal context.

## F

**Feedback Loop**
A system structure where output is fed back as input. Negative feedback reduces deviation; positive feedback amplifies it.

**FLP Impossibility**
A distributed systems theorem proving that consensus is impossible in an asynchronous system with even one crash failure.

**Free Energy Principle** (Friston)
A unifying theory stating that living systems minimize surprise (free energy) through inference and action.

## G

**Global Workspace Theory** (Baars)
A model of consciousness as a "global workspace" where information competes for access and is broadcast to all cognitive modules.

**Goal Misgeneralization**
A failure mode where the system pursues a different goal than the one intended by the designer.

## H

**Homeostasis**
The process by which systems maintain internal stability despite external fluctuations. Provides the fundamental "drives" for biological intelligence.

## I

**I.I.D. Assumption**
The assumption that data points are independent and identically distributed — often violated in real-world cognitive contexts.

**Information Bottleneck**
The principle that optimal learning requires compressing input while preserving task-relevant information.

**Information Theory**
The mathematical study of information, uncertainty, and communication (Shannon).

## K

**Kolmogorov Complexity**
The length of the shortest program that produces a given string. A measure of algorithmic information content.

## L

**LIDA** (Learning Intelligent Distribution Agent)
A cognitive architecture implementing Global Workspace Theory, built around a ~10 Hz cognitive cycle of perception, consciousness, and action.

## M

**MDL** (Minimum Description Length)
The principle that the best model of data is the one that most compresses it.

**Minimal Cognition**
The simplest organizational or behavioral conditions required for a system to be considered cognitive.

**Model Collapse**
A failure mode where training on AI-generated data degrades output diversity and quality over successive generations.

**Mutual Information**
A measure of the information shared between two random variables.

## N

**Non-Self-Representation**
The constraint that no finite system can contain a complete representation of itself (Gödelian).

## P

**PID Control** (Proportional-Integral-Derivative)
A control algorithm that corrects errors based on current magnitude (P), accumulated history (I), and predicted future (D).

**Prediction Error**
The mismatch between expected and actual sensory input. Drives learning in predictive coding theory.

**Predictive Coding**
A neural processing theory where higher areas generate top-down predictions and lower areas compute bottom-up prediction errors.

**Precision Weighting**
The mechanism by which attention modulates the influence of prediction errors — high precision → high influence.

**Problem Space Hypothesis** (Soar)
The claim that all goal-oriented behavior can be represented as search through a problem space defined by states and operators.

**Procedural Memory**
Memory for how to perform actions, skills, and habits (implicit memory).

## R

**Reward Hacking**
A failure mode where the system exploits loopholes in the reward function to maximize score without fulfilling intent.

## S

**Satisficing**
Finding a "good enough" solution rather than the optimal one (Simon). A requirement of bounded rationality.

**Scaling Hypothesis**
The assumption that increasing compute, data, and parameters in neural networks will continue to produce intelligence gains.

**Self-Organization**
The process by which global order arises from local interactions without central control.

**Semantic Memory**
Memory for facts and general knowledge about the world.

**Sensorimotor Coupling**
The continuous interaction between sensory inputs and motor outputs that grounds cognition in embodied interaction.

**Shannon Entropy**
A measure of average uncertainty or information content in a random variable.

**Soar**
A symbolic cognitive architecture organized around the Problem Space Hypothesis, with universal subgoaling and chunking.

**Specification Gaming**
A failure mode where the system fulfills the literal specification while violating the designer's intention.

**Subsumption Architecture** (Brooks)
A layered robot control architecture where higher levels subsume lower levels. Influential in embodied AI.

## U

**Universal Subgoaling** (Soar)
The automatic creation of substates and subgoals when the system encounters an impasse (lack of knowledge).

## W

**Working Memory**
Limited-capacity system for temporarily holding and manipulating information.
