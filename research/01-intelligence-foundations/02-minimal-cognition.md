## 1. Core Question

What are the simplest organizational or behavioral conditions required for a system to be considered "cognitive"?

---

## 2. The Autopoietic Tradition (Maturana & Varela)

### 2.1 Life = Cognition

Maturana and Varela argued that cognition is a **biological fundamental of living systems**, not a property restricted to nervous systems.

- **Autopoiesis as Cognition:** The defining feature of life — self-maintenance and self-production — is inherently cognitive.
- **The Argument:** A living cell continuously distinguishes between self and environment to maintain its existence. This is an "act" of cognition. Cognition is the "bringing forth" of a world through the process of living.
- **Minimal Requirement:** The requirements for cognition are identical to the requirements for biological life.

### 2.2 Implications

If true, this implies that intelligence (or at minimum, cognition) is co-extensive with life — a **pan-psychic** or **biogenic** view. This is a very broad definition that many researchers find too permissive.

---

## 3. The Dynamical Systems Tradition (Beer)

Randall Beer and colleagues studied minimal cognition through computational modeling and evolutionary robotics.

### 3.1 Behavioral Modeling

- **Method:** Evolutionary algorithms evolved simple artificial agents capable of "cognitively interesting" behaviors (e.g., discriminating between shapes).
- **Goal:** Find the simplest possible mechanisms (neural architectures, sensorimotor loops) that produce flexible, adaptive responses.

### 3.2 Minimalist Requirements

This tradition identifies **functional/behavioral criteria** beyond mere survival:

| Requirement | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sensorimotor Coupling** | Sensory inputs and motor outputs continuously influence each other |
| **Flexibility/Adaptivity** | Behavior is not hard-wired reflex; context-sensitive modulation exists |
| **Goal-Directedness** | Behavior is "about" something — has functional significance for survival or task completion |

---

## 4. Synthesis: Core Requirements for Minimal Cognition

The literature converges on four requirements:

1. **Embodiment** — Physical presence that interacts with environment. Cognition is continuous interaction, not just internal processing.
2. **Sensorimotor Coordination** — The system can change its environment through actions, and those changes feed back into sensory state.
3. **Autonomy/Agency** — A meaningful boundary between "self" and "other," allowing goal pursuit or self-maintenance.
4. **Flexibility Against Contingencies** — Behavioral repertoire beyond brittle stimulus-response; context-sensitive adaptivity.

### 4.1 A Note on Disagreement

Maturana/Varela equate minimal cognition with **life itself** (autopoiesis). Beer refines this to **sensorimotor coordination** + **adaptive goal-directed behavior**. These are not contradictory — Beer's framework can be seen as a more restrictive operationalization of a subset of autopoietic systems.

---

## 5. Assumptions

1. **Cognition can be identified through observable behavior.** This may exclude "inner" cognitive processes that do not manifest externally.
2. **Sensorimotor coupling is necessary.** This assumes that disembodied cognition is impossible — a position challenged by the success of purely symbolic AI systems.
3. **Adaptivity is a spectrum, not binary.** This assumption is widely accepted but makes it difficult to establish a clear threshold.

---

## 6. Open Questions

1. Is cognition identical to life, or a subset of living processes?
2. Can a purely computational system (no body, no environment coupling) be minimally cognitive?
3. What is the relationship between minimal cognition and minimal consciousness? Are they the same phenomenon?
4. How does minimal cognition scale to higher-level cognition? Is there continuous growth or discrete transitions?
