## 1. Core Question

Is intelligence fundamentally linguistic, or is language a specialized module layered atop more primitive cognitive machinery? The evidence strongly supports the latter.

---

## 2. Key Perspectives

### 2.1 Embodied Cognition

The **Embodied Cognition** framework argues that cognitive processes are rooted in the body's interactions with the environment — not in abstract symbol manipulation.

- **Physical Grounding:** Intelligence is scaffolded by morphology (shape, physical capabilities). An agent's body constrains and directs its cognitive development.
- **Rejection of Computationalism:** Many embodied theories reject the view that intelligence requires internal symbolic representations (a "language of thought"). Adaptive behavior can emerge directly from the **coupling** of agent to environment without high-level abstraction.
- **Key Source:** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — *Embodied Cognition*.

### 2.2 Animal Cognition

Research in animal cognition demonstrates intelligence in organisms lacking human-level language.

- **Definition:** Intelligence is the capacity for **adaptive information processing** — acquiring, processing, and utilizing environmental information for survival-relevant responses.
- **Neurological Dissociation:** Studies (MIT EvLab, Fedorenko et al.) show that domain-general intelligence (fluid reasoning, executive function) is neurologically distinct from language-processing networks. Patients with global aphasia (complete loss of language) retain sophisticated non-verbal reasoning, tool use, and navigation.

### 2.3 Defining Non-Linguistic Intelligence

Across disciplines, intelligence without language is defined by:

| Trait | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Interactional Agency** | Manipulating environment to satisfy needs (not manipulating symbols to describe it) |
| **Sensorimotor Coupling** | Perception as active sampling through movement; thinking is doing |
| **Robustness in Complexity** | Maintaining stability and achieving goals despite changing surroundings |

---

## 3. Operational Definition

**Intelligence (non-linguistic) = the capacity of an embodied agent to successfully participate in its environment through adaptive sensorimotor coordination, maintaining viability against perturbations.**

This definition is deliberately minimal: it does not require language, consciousness, or explicit reasoning.

---

## 4. Assumptions

1. **Embodiment is necessary for intelligence.** This is a working hypothesis, not proven. [UNVERIFIED — counterarguments exist in pure mathematical reasoning traditions]
2. **Language is a specialized module, not the substrate of intelligence.** Supported by neurological dissociation evidence.
3. **Intelligence is measurable through behavioral adaptivity.** This assumes observability is sufficient, which may exclude forms of intelligence that do not manifest behaviorally.

---

## 5. Open Questions

1. Can a purely symbolic system (no embodiment) exhibit genuine intelligence? (The Chinese Room argument vs. functionalism debate.)
2. Is there a continuous spectrum from minimal to full intelligence, or discrete thresholds?
3. Does non-linguistic intelligence require consciousness, or are the two separable?
